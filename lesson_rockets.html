1. What is a Rocket?

Rockets move by pushing gas out very fast. The push back makes the rocket go up. This is called thrust.

2. Stages

Big rockets use stages. The first stage lifts the rocket, then drops away to save mass. Upper stages keep going to space.

3. How Rockets Steer

Engines can swivel to point the thrust. Small thrusters and fins help guide the rocket safely.

4. Journey to the Moon

First the rocket reaches Earth orbit. Then a burn sends the ship towards the Moon. It slows into Moon orbit and then lands.

5. What a Moon Base Needs

Humans need air, water, food, the right pressure, and safe temperature. We also need power and strong shelters.

6. Staying Safe

Radiation from space can be dangerous. Walls covered with Moon dust (regolith) help protect people inside. Suits protect outside.

7. Power and Water

Solar panels make electricity. Some plans use small nuclear power. Water ice at the Moon’s poles could be used for drinking and fuel.

8. Life Support

Air is cleaned and reused. Water is recycled. Plants can help make oxygen and food.

9. Orbits and Delta‑v

Getting to space needs speed as well as height. Extra speed changes orbit. This energy change is called delta‑v.

10. Re‑entry and Heat Shields

Coming home, the ship hits air and gets hot. Heat shields protect it by burning away and spreading heat.

11. Habitats and Modules

Moon bases can be modules joined by tunnels. Airlocks keep the inside safe when people go out in suits.

12. Comms and Navigation

Satellites and ground stations pass messages. Antennas on the base talk to Earth and to rovers.

Did you know?

The Moon’s gravity is about 1/6 of Earth’s. Astronauts can jump higher!

Fun fact!

Water ice in shadowed craters can be split into oxygen and hydrogen—air and rocket fuel!
